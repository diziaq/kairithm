## Ask

A stakeholder wants a delivery date for a feature that depends on a system your team has never
worked with. They want the number before the end of the day. What do you give them?

## Tests

Whether the candidate can express genuine uncertainty in a form a business can plan against,
instead of either refusing to commit or inventing a number they will be held to.

## Ideal minimal answer

Asks what decision the number is being used for and answers that. Gives a range with its
assumptions attached, says plainly which part is a forecast and which part they will commit to,
holds that line when pushed, and sets an agreed rhythm for re-forecasting so a slip is news
early.

## Listen for

- Asks what the date is for — a contract, a campaign, a dependency, or a wish to feel organised
- Gives a range with the assumptions attached, and says what would narrow it
- Names the specific unknown and proposes a short, bounded piece of work to reduce it, with a
  date for the estimate rather than for the feature
- Distinguishes a forecast from a commitment out loud, and is explicit about which they are
  giving
- Offers a smaller first delivery that can be dated with more confidence
- Says what they will do when the estimate moves, and when the stakeholder will hear about it

## Strong signals

- Asks what decision the stakeholder is making with the number, and answers that instead
- Is willing to commit to a date for a reduced scope, and says exactly what is in it
- Refuses to let the range be collapsed to its optimistic end in the retelling, and puts it in
  writing
- Has a view on what this team's estimates have historically been worth, and uses it

## Weak signals

- Gives a single confident number to end the conversation
- Refuses to say anything without a full analysis
- Doubles a guess and offers that as rigour, with no assumptions stated
- Agrees to a date set by the stakeholder and plans to explain later

## Answer bands

### mid

- Gives a rough range rather than one number.
- Says it will firm up once the team has looked at the unfamiliar system.
- Asks for time to investigate before committing.

### senior

- Separates what is understood from what is not, and dates them differently.
- Proposes a short, bounded investigation with a fixed end and a commitment to estimate after it.
- States assumptions alongside the range so that a broken assumption is a visible event.
- Offers a reduced first delivery that can carry a firmer date.

### lead

- Asks what the number is being used for and solves that problem rather than the request.
- Says plainly which part is a forecast and which part they will commit to, and holds that line
  when pushed.
- Sets an agreed rhythm for re-forecasting, so a slip is news early rather than a surprise late.
- Uses the team's track record rather than optimism, and can defend the range with it.

## Follow-ups

- They say they hear you, and then tell the board the earliest end of your range. What do you do?
  probes: whether a range survives retelling, and whether they correct it in writing
- Two weeks in, the part nobody had done before turns out to be twice the size you feared. When
  do they hear about it?
  probes: re-forecasting as a scheduled act rather than a confession
- They cannot move the date and will not cut anything. What do you say?
  probes: whether they can name the only remaining variable rather than accept an impossible
  commitment
