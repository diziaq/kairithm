## Ask

You estimated a task at two days. It is now day nine and it is still not finished. What did you
do on day three, and what do you do today?

## Tests

Whether the candidate notices a slipping estimate early and raises it, instead of absorbing the
overrun in silence and hoping to catch up.

## Ideal minimal answer

Says day three was the moment to speak, names the specific part that turned out bigger rather
than saying it was harder, and today gives a revised estimate — asking for help if it is still
uncertain — instead of promising tomorrow again.

## Listen for

- Says that day three was the moment to speak, not day nine
- Tells whoever is depending on it as soon as the estimate stops being true, not when the work
  ends
- Can say concretely what turned out to be bigger than expected, rather than "it was harder"
- Gives a new estimate with what it is based on, and what is still unknown
- Offers options: a smaller version, help, dropping part of the scope
- Asks whether the task is still worth doing at the new price

## Weak signals

- Kept quiet and worked evenings to catch up
- Says they will be done tomorrow, having said that on days four through eight
- Blames the estimate for being wrong, with nothing learned from it
- Waits for someone to ask

## Answer bands

### weak

- Says nothing until asked, or promises tomorrow again.
- Cannot say what part of the work was underestimated.
- Treats the overrun as normal and unremarkable.

### junior

- Says they should have flagged it as soon as it went over.
- Explains what turned out to be bigger than they thought.
- Gives a revised estimate and asks for help if it is still uncertain.

### mid

- Describes a check-in against the estimate before it has been blown, not after.
- Splits what is done from what is left and estimates only the remainder, with its unknowns.
- Puts the decision in front of whoever is depending on it: continue, shrink, or stop.
- Names what they would estimate differently next time, tied to what they missed.

## Follow-ups

- Your manager's first reaction is visible irritation. Does that change what you tell them next
  time?
  probes: whether bad news travels regardless, and what they need to keep reporting honestly
- Another team has been waiting on your task since day three and nobody told them. What now?
  probes: who else is affected by a private overrun
- Today you genuinely believe you are four hours from done. Do you still raise it?
  probes: the sunk cost of nearly finished, and the reliability of that belief on day nine
